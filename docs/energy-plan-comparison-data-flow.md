# Energy Plan Comparison: Consumer Data Flow

To match you with the most accurate energy plan, both comparison websites and retail companies need to move you through a highly structured data pipeline.

Because energy pricing depends heavily on where you live, how much power you pull from the grid, and when you use it, generic quotes are rarely accurate. The actual consumer journey and the flow of required details typically follow this sequence.

## 📍 Phase 1: Location & Meter Identification (The Filter)

The flow always starts here because your geography dictates which distributors own the wires and what regulated reference prices apply.

### Postcode / Suburb

This immediately identifies your specific energy network distribution zone, such as Ausgrid or Endeavour Energy in New South Wales.

### Life Situation / Move Date

You will be asked whether you are:

- Staying at your current property and looking for a cheaper rate.
- Moving to a new home and requiring a physical connection date.

### National Meter Identifier (NMI)

Advanced tools may ask for your address to look up your NMI—a unique 11-digit number attached to your physical electricity meter. This allows them to identify your meter type instantly.

## 📊 Phase 2: Consumption Profiling (The Multipliers)

Next, the provider must determine how much energy you actually use. Companies usually collect this data in one of three ways, ranging from the most accurate to a rough estimate.

### Option A: Smart Meter / Data Sharing (Most Accurate)

Thanks to Australia's Consumer Data Right (CDR), you can securely sign in and grant permission for the comparison tool to retrieve your historical usage data directly from your current provider.

### Option B: Recent Bill Upload

You upload a PDF of your latest bill or manually enter your usage numbers—for example, `12.5 kWh per day` during the previous quarter.

### Option C: Household Estimate (Least Accurate)

If you do not have a bill, you will be asked profiling questions such as:

- How many people live in the house?
- Do you have a swimming pool, ducted air conditioning, or an electric vehicle (EV)?
- Are you usually home during the day or mostly at night?

## ☀️ Phase 3: Infrastructure Constraints (The Add-ons)

Your property's physical setup changes which tariffs and pricing structures you are eligible for.

### Solar Status

You will be asked whether you have solar panels, the system size—for example, `5 kW` or `10 kW`—and sometimes your current solar feed-in tariff (FiT). This helps the provider recommend a plan that pays appropriately for energy exported back to the grid.

### Controlled Load

The provider checks whether you have a separate, dedicated meter for high-consumption appliances such as an electric hot-water storage tank or underfloor heating.

## 👤 Phase 4: Lead Generation & Value-Add (The Capture)

After the system calculates your potential savings against the Reference Price—Default Market Offer (DMO) or Victorian Default Offer (VDO)—the flow transitions into capturing your information to monetize the lead.

### Contact Information

To unlock the full breakdown or save the quote, you may need to provide:

- Name
- Email address
- Phone number

### Concession Cards

You will be asked whether you hold a Pensioner Concession Card or Health Care Card. Eligible cards can unlock state-government energy rebates that retailers must apply to your bill.

### Consent Checkboxes

You will be asked to agree to privacy policies. On comparison websites, this consent may also authorize phone agents to contact you and help complete the sale.

## 🔄 Summary: Consumer-to-Lead Flow

```text
[Postcode / Address]
        ↓
[Upload Bill / Complete Usage Profile]
        ↓
[Select Solar / EV Options]
        ↓
[View Savings Against Reference Price]
        ↓
[Enter Name / Phone / Email to Switch or Save]
```
